# Wanderply — Voiceover Read Sheet

Clean read script for a voice artist. **No screen directions** — just the lines,
in order, with delivery notes. Record each block as a separate take; the editor
syncs to picture. Companion to `PROMO_VIDEO_SCRIPTS.md` (which has the visuals).

**Brand name:** *Wanderply* — say it "WAN-der-ply" (rhymes with "ply", like plywood).
Never "Wander-plee" or "Wander-play."
**URL read:** "Wanderply dot com."
**Overall voice:** warm, confident, a little playful. A friend who's good at this,
not a corporate announcer. Smile in the voice. Don't oversell — the product is the flex.

---

## Session setup (read once to the engineer)
- Record at 48kHz / 24-bit, mono, dry (no processing).
- Leave ~1s of room tone at head and tail of every take.
- Give **3 reads** of each line: (1) as written, (2) a touch faster/tighter,
  (3) a warmer/slower alt. Editor picks per cut.
- Pace targets below are guides — natural delivery beats hitting the clock.

---

## BLOCK 1 — Hero / 15s energy (fast, bright, ~2.5 words/sec)
Punchy. Land each line like a hook. Tiny lift on "one sentence."

1. "Plan a whole trip in one sentence."
2. "Wanderply builds the itinerary for you—"
3. "—day by day, ready to go."
4. "Wanderply dot com."

*Alt tag for social:* "A whole trip, planned from one sentence. Wanderply."

---

## BLOCK 2 — 30s confident (balanced, ~2 words/sec)
Assured, easy. Slight wry smile on "twenty tabs."

1. "Planning a trip used to mean twenty tabs."
2. "Wanderply just needs one sentence — where, when, and how you like to travel."
3. "Then it builds the whole itinerary. Day by day, ready to go."
4. "Every stop mapped, with a reason it's worth your time."
5. "Trails, packing lists, booking deals — all built in."
6. "Wanderply. Your trip, planned."

---

## BLOCK 3 — 60s warm (relaxed, conversational, ~1.8 words/sec)
Let it breathe. This is the storytelling read. Small pauses at the em-dashes.

1. "Planning a trip used to mean twenty tabs and a spreadsheet."
2. "Wanderply just needs one sentence."
3. "Tell it where you're going, when, who's coming, and how you like to travel."
4. "Then it does the hard part: a real, day-by-day plan with timing, directions, and local picks."
5. "Every day mapped. Every stop with a reason it's worth your time."
6. "Add trails, get a packing checklist, find booking deals with member rates built in."
7. "Share it with anyone, and take it anywhere — even offline."
8. "Wanderply. Your trip, planned."

---

## BLOCK 4 — Short-form / social hooks (playful, first-person, casual)
These are POV/meme-style reads — loose, like talking to a friend. Don't over-enunciate.

1. "POV: you're too lazy to plan a trip… so you let AI do it." *(dry, then a beat)* "…that's genuinely a good plan?"
2. "I never forget a charger again." *(deadpan-proud)*
3. "Delayed-flight starter pack." *(flat, meme cadence)*
4. "Me and my group chat finally agreeing on something." *(amused)*
5. "Roadtrip — but make it a guided tour."

---

## BLOCK 5 — Taglines / end cards (record clean, isolated)
Give a few flavors of each — neutral, warm, punchy.

1. "Wanderply. Your trip, planned."
2. "Your trip, planned." *(name will be on-screen)*
3. "Wanderply dot com."
4. "Describe it. Wanderply builds it."
5. "Saved for offline. Ready anywhere."

---

## Pronunciation & do-not list
- **Wanderply** = WAN-der-ply. (One more time: -ply like *plywood*, not *reply*.)
- **AllTrails** = "all trails," two clear words.
- Don't say "revolutionary," "powered by AI," or "seamless." Say what it does.
- Don't rush the product-name reads — the name is the thing to remember.
