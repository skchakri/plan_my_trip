There's a specific kind of panic that hits when you round a bend into a national park, the signal drops to nothing, and the plan you spent an evening building is a spinning wheel. We've been there. So Wanderply is built to work exactly when the internet doesn't.

## Your plan is cached the moment you open it

Wanderply is a progressive web app. The first time you view a trip page **online**, the service worker quietly stores it. After that, it's available **offline** — in a tunnel, on a plane, deep in a canyon. Your day-by-day plan, your checklist, the whole thing. No "you're offline" wall, just the plan you already loaded.

You can also pin it to your home screen like a real app — there are shortcuts straight to "All trips" and "New trip" — so it opens instantly without hunting through a browser.

### Save your trivia for the road, too

Heading somewhere with no signal for a while? On the [Travel Trivia](/quizzes) page there's a **"Save all decks for offline"** button. Tap it once and all sixteen quiz decks — flags, capitals, logos, and the rest — download for the drive, images and all. Perfect for a long stretch of highway.

## Weather that's honest about the future

Every trip shows per-day weather for your dates, and it's careful about what it actually knows:

- **Within about two weeks?** You get the live forecast.
- **Further out?** You get *typical conditions* — the real average for those calendar dates over the last three years, clearly labeled as typical, not a fake forecast.
- **A past trip?** You get the recorded actuals.

It's the difference between "pack for what the weather might be" and a made-up number. When we can't get it, the panel just quietly disappears rather than showing you nonsense.

## The packing list that builds itself

Every plan comes with a **checklist**, generated from the trip itself and sorted into sections. Open it on your phone, tap items as you pack, and watch the progress bar fill. You can add your own rows inline, and assign items to specific people — genuinely useful when four travelers are splitting the gear.

Like everything else, it's cached, so you can check off "sunscreen" and "trailhead permit" whether or not you've got signal at the campsite.

## Why we obsess over this

Plenty of apps are beautiful on the couch and useless on the road. The whole reason Wanderply exists is the road — the part where the phone is mounted on the dashboard, the kids are asking how much longer, and there's no bar in sight. If the plan doesn't work *there*, it doesn't work.
