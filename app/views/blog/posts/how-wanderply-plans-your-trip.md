Most "AI trip planners" hand you a wall of text and call it a plan. Wanderply is built the opposite way: it asks the handful of questions that actually change an itinerary, then builds you a structured, day-by-day plan you can edit, share, and take on the road offline.

Here's the whole flow, start to finish.

## Step 1 — Where, when, and who

You give Wanderply a **destination**, optionally an **origin** (if you're driving), your **dates**, and how many people are coming. That's the skeleton. If you're arriving from a quiz or a road-trip guide, the destination is often already filled in for you.

## Step 2 — The levers that actually matter

This is where Wanderply is different. Instead of a blank prompt box, you set the four things that genuinely reshape a trip:

- **Pace** — relaxed, balanced, or packed. This decides how many stops land in a day.
- **Budget** — shoestring to luxury. This changes the kind of places we suggest, not just the price tags.
- **Preferences** — free-form: dietary needs, accessibility, must-dos, hard avoids. Type it like you'd tell a friend.
- **Transport** — your own car, a rental, flying, or a mix. If you're driving with an origin, Wanderply plans **famous stops along the route** as timed activities, not just the endpoints.

### Must-includes: your non-negotiables

The single most-requested feature. Add anchors like *"Disneyland — 2 days"* or *"Hoover Dam"* and Wanderply schedules **every one of them**, honoring the durations you stated. As you type, a live summary on the right updates so you can see the trip taking shape.

## Step 3 — Highlights, pre-matched

Before the plan is built, Wanderply researches the destination's highlights and shows them as cards. If one of your must-includes fuzzy-matches a highlight — you typed "Disney," we found "Disneyland Park" — it's **pre-selected and badged** so you're not picking the same thing twice. Anything of yours we couldn't match to a card is listed in an "Already in your plan" strip so nothing gets lost.

## Step 4 — The build

Hit build and Wanderply assembles a full itinerary: real days, real activities, verified coordinates (we geocode every stop, because language models hallucinate map pins), a packing checklist, and a clean markdown plan you can read like a guidebook. The plan is viewable in under a minute; the spoken narration for each stop fills in shortly after, off to the side, so you're never staring at a spinner.

## After the build: it's yours to shape

A plan is a starting point, not a verdict.

- **The concierge** lets you ask for changes in plain language — *"make day two more relaxed,"* *"swap the museum for something outdoors"* — and it proposes edits you **approve with one tap** before anything changes. The AI never silently rewrites your trip.
- **Share it** by email. Everyone who's coming sees the same live plan, and each person can rename the trip just for themselves without touching what anyone else sees.
- **Link your trails.** Drop in an [AllTrails](https://www.alltrails.com) URL and Wanderply pulls the trailhead's elevation and distance into the plan.

## Two other ways to start

Not every trip is a week away.

- **Day trips** — tell Wanderply your home base and what you're into, and it researches nearby ideas and builds a one-day plan.
- **[Curated road-trip guides](/road-trips)** — hand-built routes like San Francisco → Vegas or Denver → Moab, ready to open in the planner with a tap.
