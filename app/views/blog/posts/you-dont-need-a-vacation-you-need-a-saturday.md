There's a specific kind of Friday-night conversation that happens in a lot of households. Someone says *"we should do something tomorrow."* Everyone agrees. Then someone has to figure out *what*, and the figuring-out is just enough work that by 9pm the plan has quietly dissolved into "maybe next weekend."

The trip that dies there isn't a two-week vacation. It's a day trip — the cheapest, easiest, most repeatable kind of travel there is. And it keeps dying because the effort-to-trip ratio feels wrong: an hour of research for six hours of outing.

We built Wanderply's day-trip planner for exactly that Friday night.

![A lakeside cabin below the mountains](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1470770841072-f978cf4d019e?w=1200&q=80)

## Start from where you are, not where you're going

A normal trip starts with a destination. A day trip starts with a *radius*. You don't know where you're going — that's the whole problem.

So the planner asks for your home base and when you want to leave and be back, then goes and does the research part for you: it looks at what's actually within reach, blends a curated catalog of places with fresh AI research, and — this is the part we're most proud of — **verifies the coordinates of every suggestion against a real geocoder**. AI models are famously confident about map pins that don't exist. We don't let one within fifty miles of your Saturday.

What comes back is a short, ranked list of real ideas: the lake town, the canyon overlook, the weird little museum you've driven past for years. Each with honest drive times from *your* front door.

## Tap the ones that sound good

From there it works like the full trip wizard, just compressed. Pick the ideas that spark something, hit build, and the planner assembles them into a single timed day — departure, stops in a sensible order, lunch where lunch should be, and the drive home before you're tired of the car.

You get the same things a multi-day Wanderply trip gets: a readable plan, per-stop notes, spoken narrations that play like a passenger-seat tour guide, and a checklist so the sunscreen actually makes it into the car.

![A wooden boat on an alpine lake](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1476514525535-07fb3b4ae5f1?w=1200&q=80)

## The math that changed our weekends

Here's the reframe that got us: a vacation is something you take twice a year. A Saturday happens **fifty-two times**. If planning friction eats even half of them, that's twenty-six small adventures a year that never happened — not because you couldn't afford them, but because nobody wanted to open twelve tabs at 8pm on a Friday.

Two minutes of tapping is a price a tired Friday brain will actually pay.
