The mistake first-timers make in Yellowstone is planning by *attraction* — Old Faithful, Grand Prismatic, the Canyon, the bison — and then discovering that those four things are two hours apart on roads where a single elk can stop traffic for twenty minutes.

Plan by **loop** instead. The park's **Grand Loop Road** is a figure eight — a **Lower Loop** and an **Upper Loop** joined in the middle — and each day should live on one part of it.

## Where to sleep (decide this first)

- **West Yellowstone, MT** — the practical base: most lodging, gas, groceries; 30 min to the geyser basins. Best for the Lower Loop.
- **Gardiner, MT** (north entrance) — closest to Mammoth and Lamar Valley wildlife. Best for the Upper Loop.
- **Inside the park** (Old Faithful Inn, Canyon Lodge, Lake) — book *months* ahead, but you wake up where the day starts. If you can, do it.

The itinerary below assumes **West Yellowstone**, which is where most people end up. Adjust the loop order if you're in Gardiner.

## Day 1 — Lower Loop, west side: geysers

**Be at Old Faithful by 8am.** Not because the eruption is better early — it's the same — but because the boardwalks around it (**Upper Geyser Basin**) are empty and you'll get **Grand**, **Castle**, or **Riverside** geysers to yourself. Check the predicted times at the visitor center and walk the basin between them.

Then drive north through the geyser corridor:

- **Grand Prismatic Spring** — skip the boardwalk crowds and take the **Fairy Falls trailhead** to the **overlook** (about 1.5 miles round trip). It's the only place you actually see the color.
- **Fountain Paint Pot** — mud pots and a small geyser that goes off constantly. Fifteen minutes, worth it.
- **Firehole Lake Drive** — a one-way side road most people miss.

Late afternoon, either drive down to **West Thumb Geyser Basin** on the lake (hot springs on the lakeshore) or head back to West Yellowstone. Sunset back at Old Faithful is quiet and lovely.

## Day 2 — Lower Loop, east side: canyon and valley

Drive in via Madison and go **east** to **Canyon Village**.

- **Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone** — do the **South Rim** first: **Artist Point** (the classic Lower Falls view) and, if knees allow, **Uncle Tom's Trail** (steep stairs to spray-level with the falls). Then the **North Rim Drive** for **Brink of the Lower Falls**.
- **Hayden Valley** — drive it slowly in the late afternoon. This is where you'll see **bison** herds crossing the road, and possibly bears from a distance. Pull fully off the road, stay in the car near bison, and be patient. The "bison jam" is the whole point.
- **Mud Volcano / Dragon's Mouth Spring** — five minutes, and gloriously gross.

Loop back via **Yellowstone Lake** and West Thumb if you skipped it yesterday.

## Day 3 — Upper Loop: Mammoth and Lamar

Longest driving day — leave early.

- **Norris Geyser Basin** — the hottest, weirdest basin in the park. Do the **Porcelain Basin** loop.
- **Mammoth Hot Springs** — the travertine terraces. Drive the **Upper Terrace loop**, walk the lower boardwalks. Elk wander the village lawns.
- **Lamar Valley** — the *reason* to do the Upper Loop. It's the best wildlife-watching in the lower 48: bison, pronghorn, and — at dawn or dusk with binoculars — wolves. If you can, flip the day: Lamar at dawn, then Mammoth, then Norris on the way back.

Return south via **Tower–Roosevelt** and **Dunraven Pass** if it's open (check — it closes in bad weather), or back the way you came.

## The rules that keep the trip on schedule

1. **Wildlife jams are the schedule.** Budget an extra hour on any drive across Hayden or Lamar.
2. **Be moving by 7am.** The park's tour buses arrive at the big stops around 10. Everything before that is yours.
3. **25 yards from bison and elk, 100 from bears and wolves.** Not a suggestion — people are gored every year for a selfie.
4. **Signal is nearly nonexistent** inside the park. Everything you'll need — the map, the plan, the geyser predictions you looked at that morning — has to be on the phone before you enter.
