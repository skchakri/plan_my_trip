Every "Mighty 5 in 5 days" article makes the same mistake: it treats the parks as boxes to tick and the driving as dead time. Utah's five national parks are strung along **~600 miles of some of the best road in America**, and the loop only works if you give it a full week and drive it in the right direction.

This is the itinerary we'd hand a friend. Seven days, one loop, no doubling back, and one *good* hike per park instead of six rushed ones.

## The route, in the right order

Fly into (or start from) **Las Vegas** or **Salt Lake City** — both work — and drive the loop **counter-clockwise**: Zion → Bryce → Capitol Reef → Canyonlands → Arches → back. Going this way puts the two easiest parks first while you're still adjusting to the heat, and saves the Moab pair — the ones people fall in love with — for the end.

- **Vegas start:** Vegas → Zion is ~2.5 hours. Loop ends in Moab, then it's ~6.5 hours back to Vegas (or fly out of Salt Lake, ~4 hours).
- **SLC start:** SLC → Zion is ~4.5 hours; the loop ends in Moab, ~4 hours from SLC. The tidiest loop.

If you're driving from further afield, our [Salt Lake City to Las Vegas route](/road-trips/salt-lake-city-to-las-vegas) and [Denver to Moab route](/road-trips/denver-to-moab) both feed straight into this loop.

## Day 1–2 — Zion

**Sleep:** Springdale (right at the gate) or Hurricane / St. George if you're saving money.

Zion is a shuttle park from spring through fall — you leave the car in Springdale and ride in. Day 1, arrive by early afternoon, ride the shuttle to the end (**Temple of Sinawava**) and walk the paved **Riverside Walk** as a warm-up. Day 2 is your one big hike:

- **The Narrows** (bottom-up, as far as you like) — wading up the Virgin River between thousand-foot walls. Rent water shoes and a stick in Springdale. Skip it if there's flash-flood risk; the visitor center posts it.
- **Angels Landing** needs a permit lottery now. If you didn't win one, hike **Scout Lookout** — same trail, same views, no chains.

Leave Zion via the **Zion–Mount Carmel Highway** (UT-9) east — the switchbacks and the tunnel are the best 12 miles of road in the park.

## Day 3 — Bryce Canyon

**Drive:** ~1.5–2 hours from Zion. **Sleep:** Bryce Canyon City or Tropic.

Bryce is high (8,000+ ft) so it's the cool break in the trip. Do the **Navajo Loop + Queen's Garden** combo (~3 miles) — you drop *into* the hoodoos, which is what makes Bryce different from every viewpoint photo. Be at **Sunrise Point** for sunrise on day 4 before you leave. It's genuinely worth the alarm.

## Day 4 — Scenic Byway 12 to Capitol Reef

**Drive:** ~2.5 hours, but budget 4. **Sleep:** Torrey.

Utah **Highway 12** between Bryce and Torrey is one of the great drives — the **Hogback** stretch near Escalante is a ridge with drop-offs on both sides. Stop in **Escalante** for lunch, then in **Boulder** at the burger-and-pie places if you're hungry again. Capitol Reef gets a late afternoon: the **Scenic Drive**, the **Fruita** orchards (pick fruit in season, pay on the honor system), and the petroglyph panel.

## Day 5 — Capitol Reef morning → Canyonlands (Island in the Sky)

**Drive:** ~2.5 hours to Moab. **Sleep:** Moab (3 nights).

Morning in Capitol Reef: hike **Hickman Bridge** (short, big payoff) or the **Grand Wash** slot. Then head east through Hanksville to Moab. If you arrive by mid-afternoon, drive up to Canyonlands' **Island in the Sky** for sunset at **Grand View Point** — it's the Grand Canyon without the crowd.

## Day 6 — Arches

**Sleep:** Moab.

Arches now uses **timed-entry reservations** in peak season — book weeks ahead. Go early. **Delicate Arch** (3 miles round trip, steep slickrock, no shade) is the hike; do it first thing or for sunset, never at 1pm. Fill the middle of the day with **Windows** and **Double Arch** (short, shady-ish), and the **Devils Garden** trail to **Landscape Arch** if you have legs left.

## Day 7 — Canyonlands proper, or Dead Horse Point

If yesterday was long, do **Mesa Arch** at sunrise (10-minute walk, the photo everyone takes) and **Dead Horse Point State Park** in the morning — smaller, quieter, arguably a better overlook than anything in Canyonlands. Then drive out.

![Tent under the Milky Way](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1517824806704-9040b037703b?w=1200&q=80)

## The three things that break this trip

1. **Heat.** May–September, midday hikes are dangerous, not just uncomfortable. Hike early, siesta, hike again at 5pm.
2. **Reservations.** Angels Landing (lottery), Arches (timed entry), and Moab lodging in spring/fall. Book the week you decide to go.
3. **Fuel and signal.** Highway 12 and the Hanksville stretch have long gaps with neither. Fill up in Torrey. Download maps *and* your plan before you leave Springdale.
