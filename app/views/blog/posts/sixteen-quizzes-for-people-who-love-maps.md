Some people relax with sudoku. Our household relaxes by arguing about whether the capital of Australia is Sydney. (It isn't. That argument is why the World Capitals deck exists.)

Wanderply has a full Travel Trivia arcade at [/quizzes](/quizzes) — sixteen decks, roughly **1,500 questions**, free, and playable without an account. You can lose an evening to it before you've planned a single trip. Plenty of people do; for many visitors, the quizzes are the first Wanderply page they ever see.

## The tour

The classics are all here: **World Capitals**, **U.S. State Capitals**, and **Guess the Flag** — every flag rendered from real flag data, not a blurry image set. From there it gets more specialized:

- **World Leaders**, **World Currencies**, and **World Languages** for the news-junkie crowd — the leaders deck even tells you the "as of" date it was curated, because presidents change.
- **Famous Landmarks** and **Continents** for the geography purists.
- **Dialing Codes** and **Internet Domains** — that last one asks which country owns a domain like `.io` or `.fj`, and it is *much* harder than it sounds.
- **National Anthems**, **National Birds**, and **National Sports**, for facts you didn't know countries made official.

![A desk globe in a warm room](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1521295121783-8a321d551ad2?w=1200&q=80)

And then there are the logo decks: **Car Logos** and **Airline Logos** show you a mark and ask whose it is, while **Famous Brands** flips the format — it names a brand and gives you four logos to choose from, in a tappable grid. That one produces the most groans per round in our testing.

Every round is ten questions drawn from a much bigger bank, so replaying a deck doesn't feel like re-taking a test you memorized. Sign in and it keeps your **personal best per deck**; stay signed out and it happily lets you play anyway.

## The whole thing works on a plane

Here's the feature we built specifically for travelers: on the quiz page there's a **"Save all decks for offline"** button. Tap it once on wifi and every deck — all sixteen, every flag and logo image included — downloads to your phone. Somewhere over the Atlantic, in airplane mode, the entire arcade still plays.

That, plus the fact that road-trip passengers needed *something* to do in the dead-signal stretches, is honestly why a trip planner has a trivia section at all. We built it for the back seat. It escaped.

## For the curious, not just the competitive

If you'd rather browse than be graded, the **Country Explorer** on the same page lets you pick any country and see its full fact sheet — capital, currency, language, anthem, national bird, motto, dish, the works. It's the quiz decks' answer key, arranged for wandering.

## Go settle an argument

Start with [Guess the Flag](/quizzes) — it's the friendliest deck and the fastest to get competitive about. And when the trivia gets you daydreaming about actually *going* somewhere, the [trip planner](/users/sign_up) is one tab away. That's not an accident.
