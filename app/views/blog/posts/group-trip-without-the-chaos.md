Planning a trip alone is easy. Planning one for a group is where friendships get tested. The itinerary lives in one person's head, the "final" plan is somewhere in a 400-message thread, and by the time you leave, half the group isn't sure what's actually happening.

Wanderply is built to be the one source of truth a group can actually agree on. Here's how it kills the chaos.

![A group of friends looking out over a valley](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1517824806704-9040b037703b?w=1200&q=80)

## One plan, shared with everyone

Build the trip once, then **share it by email** with everyone coming. They don't get a screenshot or a forwarded doc — they get the same **live plan**. When it updates, it updates for everyone. No more "wait, which version is this?"

And because not everyone thinks of a trip the same way, each person can **rename the trip just for themselves**. You call it "Sarah's Bachelorette"; your friend saves it as "Nashville May" — same trip, same plan, personal labels. Nobody's view steps on anyone else's.

## The AI does the first draft so nobody has to

The reason group plans stall is that building the first draft is work, and nobody wants to own it. Wanderply owns it. Give it the destination, the dates, the group size, and a few preferences, and it builds a real day-by-day itinerary in minutes. Now the group isn't staring at a blank page arguing — they're reacting to a solid draft, which is a *far* easier conversation.

Need to adjust it? The **concierge** takes plain-language requests — "make Saturday more relaxed," "add a group dinner spot downtown" — and proposes the change for a one-tap approval. The plan never rewrites itself behind your back.

![Friends walking a trail together](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1499591934245-40b55745b905?w=1200&q=80)

## Settling up without the awkwardness

The worst part of any group trip is the money conversation at the end. Wanderply's **expense tracking** lets everyone log what they spent as they go — the Airbnb, the rental van, the big group dinner — and it works out **who owes whom** at the end. No side spreadsheet, no one person fronting everything and chasing Venmo requests for a week.

## The little things that keep a group moving

- A shared **packing checklist** with items you can assign to specific people.
- Per-day **weather** everyone can see, so nobody shows up in shorts to a cold morning hike.
- **Member-rate booking links** — if someone in the group has Hilton Honors or a AAA membership, Wanderply surfaces the rate they qualify for. (More on [the money side here](/blog/travel-smarter-with-your-money).)

## The point

A group trip doesn't fall apart because people don't want to go. It falls apart in the *coordination* — the version control, the vague plans, the money math. Put all of that in one place everyone can see, and the trip gets to be about the trip again.
