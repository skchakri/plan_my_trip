The hardest part of a road trip isn't the planning. It's the three hours between stops when everyone's bored and the only question anyone asks starts with "how much longer." Wanderply is built to fill that time — not with a screen full of ads, but with the trip itself.

## Every stop, narrated

For the major stops in your plan, Wanderply writes a short, spoken **tour-guide narration** — the *why* behind the place. What you're about to walk into, what to look for, the one fact that makes it stick. It reads like a two-voice travel podcast, not a Wikipedia dump.

These narrations build in the background after your plan is ready, so the plan itself is never held up waiting on them. By the time you're on the road, the podcast is there. Play it as you pull into each stop and the driver gets to be a passenger for a minute.

## The Drive Co-Pilot

Mount the phone on the dash and hand the back seat something to do. The **Drive Co-Pilot** turns the trip into a game — trivia tied to where you're actually going, so the eight-year-old learns something about the next stop instead of asking about it. It's designed for a moving car: big taps, no fiddly menus, reward at the end.

## Sixteen trivia decks, free and offline

Separate from any trip, Wanderply has a whole [Travel Trivia](/quizzes) section — sixteen quick multiple-choice decks you can play in a minute:

- **World Capitals**, **U.S. State Capitals**, **Guess the Flag**
- **World Leaders**, **World Currencies**, **World Languages**, **Dialing Codes**, **Internet Domains**
- **Famous Landmarks**, **Continents**, **National Anthems**, **National Birds**, **National Sports**
- **Car Logos**, **Airline Logos**, and **Famous Brands**

Every deck scores instantly, keeps your personal best, and — as covered in [our offline post](/blog/plans-that-survive-the-drive) — downloads for the road with one tap. No sign-up required to play; make an account and your best runs stick around across all sixteen.

### Country Explorer

Not in a quizzing mood? The **[Country Explorer](/quizzes/explore)** lets you pick any country and see its whole fact sheet at once — flag, capital, anthem, national bird, currency, calling code, the works. It's the reference companion to the quizzes, and a surprisingly good way to settle a back-seat argument.

## Why bother building all this?

Because a trip is more than a list of coordinates. The stuff that makes a family remember a drive — the weird fact about the dam, the flag game that got competitive, the tour-guide voice that made a roadside stop feel like a destination — that's the actual product. The itinerary just gets you there.
