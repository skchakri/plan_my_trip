You can find a thousand "ultimate road trip packing lists" online, and they're all 200 items long because they're trying to sell you 200 things. This one is organized the way it actually matters: **by where it lives in the car**, because on day four you won't remember what you packed — only where you can reach it.

## The front seat (reachable while driving)

- **Paper map or a downloaded offline map** of the region. Not optional. Zion, Yellowstone, Big Bend, the Utah 12 — you *will* lose signal.
- **Your plan, offline.** Whatever you use, make sure the itinerary opens with airplane mode on. (Wanderply plans render offline once you've opened them — that's a design rule, not a feature.)
- **America the Beautiful pass** — one card, every national park, pays for itself on the third park. Keep it clipped to the visor with your ID.
- **Sunglasses, lip balm, hand sanitizer** — desert air is brutal.
- **A real water bottle per person** (insulated; hot cars turn plastic bottles into tea).
- **Snacks that survive heat** — nuts, jerky, dried fruit. Chocolate does not survive Moab.
- **Phone mount + a car charger with two ports.** Navigation kills batteries.
- **A small trash bag.** Sounds silly; matters by day two.

## The day pack (grab-and-go for every trailhead)

One bag, always packed, so a "let's just do this short trail" doesn't take fifteen minutes of digging.

- **2–3 liters of water capacity** (bladder or bottles) — the parks say a liter per hour in summer, and they mean it.
- **Sun hat, sunscreen, and a light long-sleeve.** Shade is rare on the Colorado Plateau.
- **A layer.** Bryce is 8,000 ft; the Grand Canyon rim is 7,000 ft; Yellowstone frosts in July. A packable fleece or windbreaker earns its place every single time.
- **Salty snacks + something sweet** for the walk back.
- **Headlamp.** Sunset hikes always end in the dark. Every time.
- **First-aid basics:** blister pads, tape, ibuprofen, tweezers (cactus).
- **Trail shoes with grip** — slickrock is slick, and canyon trails are loose. Sneakers are fine on paved rim trails; not on Delicate Arch.

## The trunk (once-a-day access)

- **A cooler** — even a cheap one — with ice from the gas station each morning. Sandwich lunch at an overlook beats a $15 park hot dog every time and saves an hour.
- **A gallon of extra water** for the car itself. Emergencies, radiator, washing hands.
- **A camp chair or two.** You'll thank yourself at sunset viewpoints and campsite dinners.
- **Duffel per person, not suitcases.** Duffels squash; suitcases don't.
- **A packing cube of "clean clothes for the last day"** you're not allowed to touch. Trust us.
- **Laundry bag** — after a week you'll want to know which pile is which.
- **Basic tools:** jumper cables, tire gauge, a small pump. Remote park roads + a slow leak = a very long day.
- **Bear spray** if you're going to Yellowstone, Glacier, or the Tetons. Buy it there (you can't fly with it), keep it *on you* on trails, not in the trunk.

## The things people forget every time

- **Cash** for honor-system fruit stands, small-town diners, and campground fee boxes.
- **A power bank.** Not for the phone in the car — for the phone on the trail when it's been navigating all day.
- **Earbuds for the drive** — or, better, something to listen to together. (Our [free travel trivia decks](/quizzes) run offline and have settled a lot of back-seat silences.)
- **A dry bag / ziploc** for phones on The Narrows or any river walk.
- **A printed copy of your reservations** — timed entries, lodging, permits. Screenshots die when the phone dies.
